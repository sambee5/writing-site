# Skip the Blurb

I’m going to be brutally honest. I never enjoyed reading.

Each time I’d open a book, I’d find myself reading the blurb, instantly closing it at the sight of a boring word.

Words on their own mean nothing. Open up a dictionary and take a look. *Capacious*——define it. You can’t define it without using another word. So what was the first word? There’s got to be one that can be interpreted without meaning.

But yes. Words have no meaning.

Yet, I still found myself at the library this summer. I don’t even know why. I still didn’t enjoy books at the time. But I was still naive, and I opened a book with a cool cover on it. Asteroids and laser beams——a combination that could never possibly fail to entertain, right?

I skipped past the blurb this time. Pictures tell a thousand words, after all.I was taken aback by the insanity of the writing. City killer? Continent destroyer? I learned something new precisely because I skipped the blurb.

You should skip the blurb too. Take a risk. **We expect a defined result all the time, wanting to understand what comes next, which leads us to be unappreciative over the uncertainty of now.**

I hate ancient thought. But I still swiftly read through the pages of the Republic by Plato. Why would I endure that suffering? I don’t know. I guess I enjoy pain.

But I *still* don’t like reading.

Although, I did not read in those moments. I discovered it. Some neural pathways in my brain were not yet connected.

Reading is looking at words on a page. We need to redefine the word. It’s got such a negative connotation among youth that makes it seem villainous, when it could really have magical abilities.

What reading really is, is something that cannot be defined. Like the first word.

There's an innate meaning to it.
