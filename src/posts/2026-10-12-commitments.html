Commitments

Every time I open up a code editor or even pick up a pencil, I marvel at the endless possibilities. It's like a canvas waiting to be painted, or a wand that could cast any spell. That idea of possibility is enticing—but it is also really scary.

Limitless control is something I feel I haven't had in much of my life. And honestly, that’s not a bad thing. It's useful to have guardrails. When first learning to ride a bike, I had training wheels. I couldn't dive headfirst into transporting myself on my own; there was just too much to handle.

As I've grown older and responsibilities have begun creeping up behind me, I realize I've forgotten that I once needed a guideline to grow off of. When I first started programming, there was always a tutorial to follow. Now, it's more like writing—perhaps with the crutch of documentation, but largely independent.

The idea of structure is comfortable because it takes a huge load off my back. Having endless freedom, by contrast, is overwhelming. I've got a bunch of ideas flowing through my head right now, and a few of them are genuinely worth pursuing. But still, the lack of structure and surplus of independence paralyze me at times. How would I even start?

Over time, the unfulfilled commitment creeps up and slowly begins to feel like a burden. I get passionate about something, chase it for a week, and then it fades away as I get distracted by the next shiny idea (though it gets done eventually). Is this just the consequence of curiosity?

I still manage to stay grounded because I do the things that I love. None of what I do is superficial—I don't think I could look at myself in the mirror if it was. At times, it gets difficult to keep up with all the activities that I willingly choose to endure, but I do them because I care enough about them to try.

This same philosophy applies to how I view the world around me right now. Lately, I've heard close friends talk about "giving up" once the second semester of senior year begins.

Why? What's the point?

Having worked hard to make use of a privileged education, there's no reason to quit right at the finish line. I take the courses I take because I genuinely care enough about the material to sign up. It's not a resume pad or a fake way to show involvement.

This all ties back to commitment. Commitment means that you care enough to show up. It shows that you still possess a sense of wonder. There's never a reason to give up, because giving up implies you never truly cared about it from the very beginning.
